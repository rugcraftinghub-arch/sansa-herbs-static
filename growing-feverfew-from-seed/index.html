What Is Feverfew?

Feverfew is a short-lived perennial herb that thrives in full sun and produces small daisy-like flowers. Traditionally used to support wellness, feverfew's leaves and flowers have been harvested for natural wellness rituals. Its compact size, pest resistance, and self-seeding ability make it ideal for any herb garden.

Native to southeastern Europe, this aromatic herb has spread globally and is especially loved by herbalists for its role in teas, tinctures, and headache relief.

Where Does Feverfew Grow Best?

Feverfew grows easily in temperate and subtropical climates. It thrives in USDA Zones 5 to 9, where winters are mild and summers are warm. The plant prefers full sun to partial shade and well-drained soil. In tropical regions, it may need a bit of afternoon shade to prevent scorching.

Even outside these zones, you can grow feverfew in containers and overwinter it indoors. It’s a hardy perennial in the right conditions and often self-seeds if allowed to flower.

Step 3: Sow and Cover the Seeds Lightly

Scatter seeds onto moist soil and press gently. Don’t bury them, they need light to germinate. Keep trays in a warm, bright location (70°F/21°C).

Germination usually occurs in 10–14 days. Mist soil regularly to keep it evenly moist.

Step 4: Transplant Carefully

Once seedlings have 2–3 sets of true leaves, transplant them into larger pots or your garden.

Spacing: 12–18 inches apart

Feverfew Plant Care Tips

  • 🌞 Full sun (6+ hours daily)
  • 💧 Water when topsoil feels dry
  • 🌿 Add compost or mulch to retain moisture and block weeds
  • 🌾 Thin self-seeded babies in early spring if they overcrowd

Feverfew is deer-resistant and may deter some insect pests. However, aphids and spider mites can occasionally show up. Neem oil or insecticidal soap can manage them.

Feverfew pairs well with calming herbs like chamomile and yarrow in your herbal tea garden.

✂️ How to Harvest Feverfew

Harvest leaves and flowers in the morning after dew dries. Use scissors to clip stems at the base.

Dry them in bunches hung upside down or use a dehydrator. Store in airtight glass jars away from light.

Feverfew may be used in tea, capsules, or salves. Always consult a health professional before herbal use.

❓ Feverfew FAQs

How long does it take feverfew to grow from seed?

Feverfew seeds typically germinate within 10 to 14 days. Once germinated, the seedlings can be transplanted and will reach flowering maturity in about 10 to 12 weeks, depending on conditions.

Can I grow feverfew in pots or containers?

Yes, feverfew grows well in pots. Use a container with good drainage and place it in full sun. Make sure to water when the topsoil feels dry and consider using compost for healthy growth.

Do I need to cover feverfew seeds when planting?

No, feverfew seeds need light to germinate. Simply press them into moist soil and leave them uncovered in a bright location until they sprout.

What can I do with harvested feverfew?

You can dry the leaves and flowers for herbal teas, make a tincture, or use them in capsules or salves. Feverfew is traditionally used for headaches, inflammation, and overall wellness support.
