Why This Tool is Essential: Consistent, gentle watering is necessary for healthy herb growth. A watering can allows for precise application of water directly to the soil, helping avoid splashing delicate leaves and preventing fungal issues. It's especially important for seedlings and potted herbs.

Potting Mixes & Raised Beds

Healthy roots start with the right growing medium. These are the soil amendments and bed setups that keep herbs productive.

Potting Soil Mix, 16 Quarts, Miracle-Gro

View on Amazon →

The right potting soil is the foundation of healthy roots. It provides balanced nutrients, good drainage, and proper aeration for container-grown herbs and raised beds. Poor-quality soil can lead to stunted growth and weak plants.

Look for an organic, nutrient-rich mix designed for containers or raised beds. A good blend should be light, well-draining, and enriched with compost or natural amendments. Avoid heavy garden soil for pots, since it can compact and suffocate roots.

We've seen firsthand how much difference quality potting soil makes. Herbs planted in a light, airy mix grow stronger, produce more leaves, and resist imbalance better than those in poor soil.

If you are starting your seeds in trays or pots before transplanting, you'll need a light, fluffy medium. Check out my Ultimate Guide to Potting Soil for the best seed-starting blends.

Organic Horticultural Perlite, 4 Quarts, Legigo

View on Amazon →

Perlite is one of the simplest ways to improve drainage in container herbs. Mixed into potting soil, it keeps roots from sitting in compacted, waterlogged mix, which is one of the fastest ways to lose an herb to root rot.

Look for a clean, lightweight perlite free of dust and debris. A little goes a long way, most mixes only need perlite worked in at about 10 to 20 percent of total volume.

We work perlite into every batch of potting mix before planting herbs in containers. It's an easy step that makes a real difference in how well roots develop.

Coco Coir Brick, Compressed Organic Coconut Fiber

Coco coir is a sustainable alternative to peat moss for potting mix and seed starting. It holds moisture evenly without becoming soggy, and it's naturally low in EC and balanced in pH, which makes it gentle on young roots.

Look for a compressed brick that expands easily with water. It should be free of added salts or synthetic kitchen garden routines, especially if you're using it for edible herbs.

We like using coco coir when starting seeds or refreshing potting mix, since it holds moisture longer than standard soil without staying wet.
