What to Expect from Feverfew Seeds

Feverfew seeds are tiny and need light to germinate. Start them indoors 6–8 weeks before your last frost or direct sow after the soil warms. Expect germination in 10 to 14 days.

Seedlings will appear feathery and delicate. Once they develop true leaves, transplant to a sunny spot with well-draining soil.

Feverfew Seed FAQs (What Gardeners Also Ask)

Can I grow feverfew from seed indoors?
Yes. Use a grow light and keep seeds moist and uncovered.
When should I plant feverfew seeds?
Start indoors 6–8 weeks before your last frost date. Outdoors, wait until frost danger has passed.
Where can I buy feverfew seeds online?
Amazon, herb-specific seed stores, and aromatic herb kits all offer options.
Are feverfew seeds hard to grow?
Not at all. They're beginner-friendly and often reseed themselves naturally.
Does feverfew need cold stratification?
No, feverfew seeds do not need cold stratification. You can sow them directly into moist soil at room temperature. They germinate best with light and warmth.
When should I sow feverfew seeds?
Sow feverfew seeds indoors 6–8 weeks before your last frost date. Outdoors, wait until the soil has warmed in spring and there is no risk of frost.
Can I grow feverfew in containers?
Yes! Feverfew does very well in pots. Use a well-draining mix, make sure the container gets at least 6 hours of sunlight, and water when the topsoil feels dry.
How do I propagate feverfew?
Feverfew is usually grown from seed, but mature plants can also be divided in spring. Simply dig up a clump, split the root ball, and replant the divisions.
When does feverfew bloom?
Feverfew typically blooms in early to mid-summer, depending on when it's sown. It produces cheerful white and yellow flowers that last through the season.
