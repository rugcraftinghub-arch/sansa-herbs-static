How to make moringa powder from fresh leaves

Gather the leaves, dry them thoroughly, and grind them into a powder for your kitchen.

By Stacie · Adapted from my Sansa Herbs growing guide

Moringa is one of the plants I have access to here in Trinidad. My growing guide already includes the steps for harvesting, drying and grinding its leaves. This page brings that method together in one place, alongside my photograph of the finished powder.

The process starts with healthy green leaves and ends with a dry powder. Drying is the stage to give your attention to: the leaves need to be crisp before they go into the grinder.

From fresh leaves to powder

What you need

  • Fresh moringa leaves
  • Clean water for rinsing
  • A clean drying tray or a food dehydrator
  • A blender or grinder suitable for dry ingredients
  • A clean, dry, airtight jar

Method

  1. Gather the leaves. Choose healthy green leaves and separate the leaflets from their stems. My original guide suggests harvesting early in the day.
  2. Rinse and drain. Rinse the leaflets thoroughly in clean water. Remove excess surface water before spreading them out to dry.
  3. Dry thoroughly. My growing guide describes a shaded, airy location or a dehydrator on a low setting. Spread the leaves out rather than leaving them in a damp pile. See the drying notes below.
  4. Check the texture. The leaves should be crisp and crumble easily before grinding.
  5. Grind in short bursts. Pulse the dried leaves in a blender or grinder instead of running it continuously. Use equipment intended for dry ingredients and follow its instructions.
  6. Pack and label. Transfer the dry powder to a clean, dry, airtight jar. Label it with the preparation date and put it in a cool, dry, dark cupboard.

Let the leaves tell you when they are ready

The original guide uses crispness as the drying endpoint rather than a fixed number of hours. Keep that distinction: temperature, humidity and the drying setup affect how long drying takes.

A shaded spot must also have good airflow and dry conditions. Shade alone is not enough. If using a dehydrator, follow its herb-drying instructions and check the leaves as they dry.

General herb-drying guidance from the National Center for Home Food Preservation also uses leaves that crumble as an endpoint. This is general herb guidance, not a moringa-specific tested processing schedule.

Keep moisture out of the jar

Store the powder tightly sealed, away from light, heat and moisture. Use a clean, dry spoon when taking some out. The jar in my photograph is clear, so a dark cupboard is a useful place to keep it.

I have not included a fixed shelf life here. Storage conditions matter, and the photograph does not establish how long a batch will keep.
