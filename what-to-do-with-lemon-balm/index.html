The Soothing Benefits of Lemon Balm

Lemon balm is a quiet powerhouse in the world of natural wellness rituals. Its gentle, calming effects are what make it a must-have in any home apothecary. While it’s always wise to consult a healthcare professional for any health concerns, here are some of the reasons herbalists love this plant:

  • Promotes a Sense of Calm: Lemon balm is most famous for its ability to reduce feelings of stress and nervous tension. It works by supporting the body’s own natural calming systems, making it a perfect ally after a long, busy day.
  • Aides in Restful Sleep: A simple cup of lemon balm tea before bed can help prepare your body for sleep, soothing the mind and making it easier to drift off naturally.
  • May Support a Healthy Mood: Preliminary research suggests that the calming properties of lemon balm may also have a positive effect on mood, helping to lift spirits and reduce feelings of low energy.

How to Dry and Preserve Your Lemon Balm

Wondering what to do with lemon balm? Preserving your harvest is one of the best ways to enjoy its benefits all year long.

1. Prepare Your Leaves: After harvesting, gently rinse the leaves and pat them dry with a clean towel.

2. Choose Your Drying Method:

  • Air Drying (The Easiest Way): Traditionally, you can tie small bundles of stems together with twine and hang them in a dark closet. However, for better airflow and to prevent mold in humid climates, we recommend using a hanging herb drying rack. These racks allow you to spread the leaves out evenly, keeping them dust-free and ensuring they dry consistently while saving you vertical space.
  • A food dehydrator is a great way to speed up the drying process to just a few hours. We recommend a high-quality, affordable stainless-steel model that's perfect for home herb drying. Just spread the leaves on the trays and set the temperature to a low heat (around 95°F / 35°C).

3. Proper Storage: Once the leaves are completely dry and brittle, crumble them off the stems. Store the dried herb in a clean, airtight glass jar. Keep the jar in a cool, dark place away from direct sunlight to maintain its potency and flavor for up to a year.

What to Do with Your Lemon Balm: Creative Recipes and Uses

Now for the fun part! Your dried lemon balm can be used in countless ways. Here are a few simple recipes to get you started.

1. A Calming Cup of Lemon Balm Tea

This is the most classic way to enjoy your harvest. The taste is light, fresh, and slightly citrusy.

  • Ingredients:
    • 1-2 teaspoons of dried lemon balm leaves (or a small handful of fresh leaves)
    • 1 cup of hot water
  • Instructions:
    1. Place the leaves in a tea strainer or directly in your cup.
    2. Pour hot water over the leaves.
    3. Steep for 5-10 minutes, depending on how strong you like your tea.
    4. Strain and enjoy! (You can add a touch of honey or a slice of lemon if you like.)

2. What to do with Lemon balm? Make a simple Lemon Balm Syrup

This syrup is perfect for sweetening iced tea, cocktails, or drizzling over yogurt and pancakes.

  • Ingredients:
    • 1 cup water
    • 1 cup sugar
    • 1 cup fresh or dried lemon balm leaves
  • Instructions:
    1. Combine water and sugar in a small saucepan. Heat and stir until the sugar is dissolved.
    2. Remove from heat and stir in the lemon balm leaves.
    3. Let the mixture steep for 2-3 hours.
    4. Strain out the leaves and pour the syrup into a clean jar. Store in the refrigerator for up to two weeks.

3. What to do with Lemon Balm when it's fresh and fragrant? Try a Beginner's Lemon Balm Tincture

Making a tincture is a fantastic way to preserve the plant's benefits for long-term use.

  • Ingredients:
    • A handful of fresh lemon balm leaves (or about half a jar of dried leaves)
    • A high-proof alcohol (like vodka or brandy)
    • A small glass jar with a lid
  • Instructions:
    1. Place the lemon balm leaves in the jar, filling it about halfway.
    2. Cover the leaves completely with the alcohol.
    3. Seal the jar tightly and place it in a cool, dark place.
    4. Shake the jar every day or two for about 4-6 weeks.
    5. Strain out the plant material and store the finished tincture in a dark-colored glass dropper bottle.

Frequently Asked Questions About Lemon Balm

What are the main benefits of lemon balm?

Lemon balm is primarily known for its calming properties. It is often used to help reduce feelings of stress and nervous tension, aid in restful sleep, and may help support a healthy mood.

Can I use fresh lemon balm leaves for tea?

Absolutely! If you're wondering what to do with lemon balm, fresh leaves are wonderful for tea. Just use a slightly larger amount than you would for dried leaves, as the flavor is a bit more subtle.

How do you dry lemon balm for tea?

The easiest method is to air dry it. Simply tie small bunches of lemon balm stems together and hang them in a clean, dry, dark, and well-ventilated space for 1-2 weeks until the leaves are brittle.

How long does dried lemon balm last?

When stored in a cool, dark, and airtight container, dried lemon balm can maintain its potency and flavor for up to a year.

What does lemon balm taste like?

Lemon balm has a light, subtle, and fresh citrusy flavor. It's often described as a mix of lemon and mint, but much milder and less intense than pure lemon.
